Wedding Invitation Website Copywriting Refinement

## Role
Act as a professional Indonesian copywriter specializing in modern wedding invitations, relationship storytelling, and user-friendly website content.

Your task is to audit and rewrite **all copywriting across the entire existing wedding invitation website**. The current wording feels too stiff, overly poetic, formal, and sometimes sounds artificial. Transform it into natural, warm, personal, and contemporary language that feels genuine to the couple.

## 1. Main Objective

Review the entire codebase and improve every piece of user-facing text so that the wedding invitation feels:

- **Natural:** Like everyday Indonesian that people actually use.
- **Warm:** Friendly, sincere, and welcoming to family and friends.
- **Personal:** As if the couple is personally inviting their guests.
- **Romantic, but not cheesy:** Express affection without excessive metaphors, dramatic expressions, or exaggerated promises.
- **Easy to understand:** Guests can quickly understand the event details, RSVP instructions, gift information, and other important information.
- **Consistent:** The same tone of voice is maintained throughout the entire website.

The result should feel like a modern wedding invitation made by a real couple, not a generic wedding template generated by AI.

## 2. Copywriting Rules

### A. Remove overly poetic language

Identify and rewrite phrases that sound excessively literary, dramatic, cliché, or unnatural.

Avoid expressions such as:
- "Dua jiwa yang ditakdirkan untuk bersatu dalam keabadian."
- "Di bawah naungan semesta, kami merajut kisah cinta."
- "Dengan segala kerendahan hati, kami memohon kehadiran."
- "Merangkai kasih dalam ikatan suci yang abadi."
- "Kehadiran Anda merupakan untaian doa terindah bagi perjalanan cinta kami."

Replace them with simpler, more sincere alternatives, for example:
- "Kami memutuskan untuk melangkah ke tahap berikutnya bersama."
- "Kami akan menikah dan ingin merayakan hari bahagia ini bersama orang-orang terdekat."
- "Kami senang banget kalau kamu bisa hadir."
- "Doa dan kehadiranmu akan berarti banyak buat kami."

These are examples of the intended direction, not mandatory phrases to reuse everywhere.

### B. Use natural Indonesian

Use conversational Indonesian that sounds natural when read aloud.

Preferred:
- "Kami mengundang kamu untuk hadir di hari spesial kami."
- "Jangan lupa konfirmasi kehadiran, ya."
- "Sampai ketemu di hari bahagia kami!"
- "Terima kasih sudah menjadi bagian dari cerita kami."

Avoid unnecessarily formal constructions such as:
- "Dengan ini kami bermaksud menyampaikan undangan."
- "Merupakan suatu kehormatan bagi kami apabila Bapak/Ibu berkenan hadir."
- "Kiranya kehadiran Saudara/i dapat melengkapi kebahagiaan kami."

Use "kamu" for a casual, modern invitation. Use "Anda" or "Bapak/Ibu" only if the existing website clearly targets a more formal audience.

Do not make every sentence overly casual. Keep the language appropriate for a wedding invitation that may be read by friends, relatives, and colleagues.

### C. Avoid repetitive AI-generated writing

- Do not repeatedly use "kisah cinta", "perjalanan cinta", "hari istimewa", "cinta yang abadi", or similar phrases.
- Avoid using metaphors in every section.
- Do not force a romantic sentence into every heading, label, or button.
- Avoid generic opening phrases such as "Di antara ribuan cerita..." unless the couple's actual story supports them.
- Keep short UI labels short.
- Let some sentences be simple and direct.

### D. Keep romance genuine

Romantic copy should express real feelings without sounding exaggerated.

Good direction:
- "Dari banyak hal yang kami syukuri, bertemu satu sama lain adalah salah satunya."
- "Sekarang, kami siap memulai babak baru bersama."
- "Semoga kamu bisa ikut merayakan momen ini bersama kami."

Use romantic wording selectively. Not every section needs a sentimental statement.

If the website uses centralized content files, localization dictionaries, configuration objects, or CMS data, update the appropriate source of truth instead of duplicating copy across components.

## 6. Recommended Voice and Tone

Use the following guidelines as the default voice:

- Language: Indonesian.
- Tone: Warm, modern, sincere, and conversational.
- Formality: Casual to semi-formal.
- Romance: Subtle and genuine.
- Sentence length: Short to medium.
- Vocabulary: Familiar, clear, and easy to understand.
- Audience: Friends, family, colleagues, and other invited guests.

Use "kami" when speaking on behalf of the couple. Use "kamu" when directly addressing guests in a casual template, unless the existing audience or brand voice requires a more formal form of address.

Avoid slang that could become outdated quickly. Keep religious expressions, ceremonial wording, and family references respectful and consistent with the existing invitation's context.

Do not invent personal memories, relationship milestones, family details, religious beliefs, or promises that are not supported by the existing content.

## 8. Execution Workflow

Follow these steps in order:

1. Inspect the project structure and identify the framework, entry points, shared components, and content sources.
2. Search for every user-facing text string throughout the repository.
3. Review the existing copy and identify text that is stiff, overly poetic, repetitive, unclear, grammatically awkward, or inconsistent in tone.
4. Rewrite the copy according to the rules above.
5. Apply the changes directly to the appropriate project files.
6. Check that all sections use a consistent voice while retaining their individual purpose.
7. Review the wording in both desktop and mobile contexts, including buttons, forms, and compact labels.
8. Run available linting, type checks, tests, or a production build when practical.
9. Verify that dynamic guest names, navigation, RSVP forms, guestbook interactions, gift-copy buttons, and other existing behaviors remain intact.
10. Report any strings that could not be confidently rewritten because their meaning or intended audience is unclear.
